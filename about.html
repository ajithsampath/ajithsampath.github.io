About me

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Geneva, working on 21 cm cosmology with the Hydrogen Intensity and Real-time Analysis eXperiment (HIRAX). I am a radio astronomer and cosmologist interested in using neutral hydrogen as a tracer of large-scale structure across cosmic time.

My research centres on the instrumentation and systematics that stand between raw interferometer data and a clean cosmological signal: characterising primary beams from electromagnetic simulations and measurements, quantifying beam chromaticity, and propagating these effects through to the 21 cm power spectrum. I have contributed to antenna characterisation and array-status work for HIRAX as the experiment has scaled toward its full array.

Before joining Geneva I completed my PhD at the University of KwaZulu-Natal in South Africa, also within the HIRAX collaboration.
